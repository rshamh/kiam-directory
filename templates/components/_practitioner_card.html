This repo adds the rail and the register's three-column placement, and nothing else. The fixed order is the point of the register: comparing five strangers should be five vertical reads, not five re-orientations. ONE MARKUP, TWO LAYOUTS — the Phase 5b search-bar rule, for the same reason. `variant` changes a modifier class and nothing else: the elements, their order and their ids are identical either way, so there is one keyboard pattern and one set of compliance rules to keep right rather than two components that drift. The register puts the rail in a third column against a hairline; the grid stacks it under the body. Both are CSS. Four things are ours and stay ours, each a compliance or performance decision the packaged partial has no way to know about: * **No contact details, and no contact button.** The packaged card renders a "Show contact" button when passed a `contact_id`; nothing here passes one and nothing may. Contact is revealed on the profile, through a POST, behind a per-IP limit — see `directory/_contact_panel.html`. The rail's affordance is therefore "View profile", which is where the reveal lives. It is deliberately the same size and weight as the reference design's contact button, because the thing a visitor is looking for is the way in to this person, and that way in is one screen further on than the reference assumes. * **`headshot_priority` and `srcset`.** Upstream's `monogram.html` renders a plain lazy `` at a fixed 56px. The first card on `/search/` is the likely LCP element and must be eager; the same card in the home-page grid sits below the fold and must not claim `fetchpriority="high"`. So the `` is ours, wearing `ds-dir-monogram` so it looks packaged. * **The badge decides for itself whether to render.** `_verified_badge.html` renders NOTHING for an unverified listing — the Phase 5 compliance finding — and carries the mandatory "What this does and does not mean" link. Upstream's card assumes a `verified_date` and renders a static span. * **"Paid placement", not "Featured".** The reference design labels the slot "Featured", which says a listing was chosen and not that it was bought. Undisclosed paid ranking breaches CAP rules (docs/content-compliance.md §6), so the pill keeps the word that discloses it and takes the reference's placement and colour instead. **Two tab stops, and the second is the badge's link rather than a contact button.** The name is a stretched link whose `::after` covers the card, so a click anywhere opens the profile while the accessible name stays "Dr Aisha Rahman" rather than the whole card read aloud. The badge's explanation link lifts back above the stretch (`.dir-practitioner__trust`, in app.css) because the policy requires the limits of the claim to be one click away from wherever the claim appears — and on a results page the claim appears twenty times. "View profile" is `aria-hidden` with `tabindex="-1"`: it duplicates a target that already covers the whole row, and eighteen cards each offering the same link twice is eighteen wasted tab stops. {% endcomment %}
