What the finished notice will cover

  • Who the data controller is, and how to contact them.
  • What is held about a listed practitioner: the profile they publish, and — separately and privately — the evidence behind their verification.
  • What is held about a visitor: no account, no profile, and no per-visitor record of what you looked at.
  • The lawful basis for each, and how consent is withdrawn.
  • How long each kind of record is kept, and what happens to evidence when a listing is removed.
  • Your rights, and how to exercise them.
  • The outcome of the data protection impact assessment.

What the site does today

These are design decisions already made, and the finished notice will describe them:

  • You do not need an account to read this directory, and there is no way to create one. Only listed practitioners and Kiam staff sign in.
  • No per-visitor analytics. Listings count how many times a page was viewed and a contact detail revealed, as daily totals. There are no visitor records behind those numbers to identify, export or delete.
  • Nothing third-party loads before you have decided about cookies. See cookies.
  • Verification evidence is stored apart from anything public, has no public address at all, and every time a member of staff opens one of those files it is recorded — who, when, and which file.
  • A practitioner can unpublish their own listing without asking anyone.
  • {% comment %} Art. 13(1)(e) — recipients have to be named, and this is a new processing operation for the DPIA. The call is server-side, so the visitor's own IP address is NOT disclosed and no cookie is involved; what is disclosed is the text they typed, which is routinely a full postcode. {% endcomment %} If you type a location when searching, that text is sent to postcodes.io (Ideal Postcodes Ltd) to turn it into a point on a map, and to suggest place names as you type. Nothing else about your search is sent, your own network address is not passed on, and no record of what you searched for is kept against you.
